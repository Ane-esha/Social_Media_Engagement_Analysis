# Social Media Engagement Analysis Using Python

## Project Overview

Cleaned a 5,000-record dataset: mean/mode imputation of missing values, duplicate and invalid-value checks, category validation, and datetime conversion.Engineered hashtag-count and engagement-score features and log transformed engagement rates to reduce the influence of extreme values.Applied descriptive statistics, group by, and correlation analysis across post type, category, country, age group, device, verification status, and sentiment; built 10 visualizations. 

# Project Workflow

- Collected the social media engagement dataset containing user details, post information, engagement metrics, and content performance data.

- Cleaned and preprocessed the dataset by handling missing values, standardizing categorical values, and converting date and time columns into appropriate formats.

- Performed exploratory data analysis (EDA) to understand the dataset and identify important patterns.

- Analyzed user demographics, including age, gender, and country.

- Examined engagement metrics such as likes, comments, shares, impressions, watch time, and engagement rate.

- Compared engagement across post types, including images, videos, reels, and text posts.

- Analyzed content categories to identify which topics generated higher engagement.

- Explored the relationship between likes, impressions, and engagement rate using correlation analysis.

- Compared engagement across countries and age groups.

- Analyzed verified and non-verified account performance.

- Examined engagement and impressions by sentiment and day of the week.

- Compared average watch time across different devices.

- Created visualizations using Matplotlib and Seaborn to present key findings and trends.

## Key Insights

- Video posts recorded the highest average engagement among the post types analyzed.
- Technology content generated the highest average engagement among the content categories.
- Brazil recorded the highest average engagement among the countries analyzed.
- Age showed almost no linear relationship with engagement.
- Verified accounts had slightly higher average engagement than non-verified accounts.
- Tuesday recorded the highest average impressions among the days analyzed.
- Watch time varied across mobile, tablet, and desktop devices.
- Correlation analysis showed that impressions and engagement rate had a weak negative relationship, while likes and engagement rate had a weak positive relationship.

## Conclusion

This project helped identify patterns in social media engagement and content performance. The findings provide insights into how post types, content categories, countries, verification status, and other factors relate to engagement. These insights can help content creators and social media teams make more informed decisions about content planning and audience engagement.
